# In praise of simple tools

An opinion I keep coming back to: the simplest tool that solves the problem
almost always wins over time.

Complex tools promise power, but they charge rent. Every extra moving part is
something to configure, debug, and remember six months from now. Simple tools
stay out of the way.

> "Perfection is achieved not when there is nothing more to add, but when there
> is nothing left to take away."

This site is an example. No build step, no framework, no database. Just HTML, a
bit of CSS, and a few lines of JavaScript to render markdown. To publish, I add
a file. That's the whole workflow.
